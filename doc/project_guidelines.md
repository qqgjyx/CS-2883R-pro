## Overview

The final project gives you a chance to explore an open research problem in computer vision and
take meaningful first steps toward solving it. Each project may be completed individually or in
pairs. The project may focus on (but are not limited to) topics discussed in class. You are not
expected to build a complete, polished system. Instead, the project will be evaluated based on the
originality of the idea, the clarity of the problem statement, the strength of the technical
approach, and the early evidence provided through preliminary experiments. The project has three
parts: a proposal, an in-class oral presentation, and a final report.

**AI Policy:** You are allowed to use AI to help with brainstorming and implementation. However,
the content of your written submissions - including the proposal and final report - must be your
own. You may use AI to polish your writing, but the original ideas and first draft must come from
you. Submissions containing major AI-generated content will receive a substantial grade deduction.
If you use AI for assistance, you must clearly acknowledge exactly what you used and how you used
it.

## Final project report

The final report should be four pages long, excluding references. It should be formatted like a
research paper using the ACM template. LaTeX is recommended but not required.

A recommended report structure is provided below. You may use it as a reference, but you are
welcome to organize your report in whatever way best suits its content.

**Title and author information.** Include the project title, your name, and email address.

**Abstract.** Provide a clear summary of the proposed research. A reader should be able to quickly
understand the project's motivation and main contribution.

**Research goal and problem statement.** Clearly explain the research goal and the open problem
your project addresses. Describe why the problem matters and why it remains unresolved. Identify
the main technical challenges and define the scope of the project.

Include at least one figure that helps explain the problem setting, shows where current methods
fail, or illustrates the intuition behind your proposed direction. Aim to demonstrate a clear
understanding of the problem's scientific, technological, and/or social importance.

**Relationship to prior work.** Discuss published research related to your project. Explain how
your idea differs from, extends, or challenges existing methods or assumptions.

**Method.** Explain how you plan to address the research problem. Describe the main idea, the key
insights behind it, and the model architecture. Include at least one figure to illustrate the
proposed method.

Also describe the results you expect. These might include qualitative and quantitative
improvements, or new capabilities made possible by the proposed method.

**Experimental results.** Present early results or analysis that show progress and support the
feasibility of your idea. Depending on the project, this could include comparisons with baseline
or published methods, ablation studies, or qualitative analysis. You may use figures, tables, or
plots to support the discussion. The experiments do not need to be complete. Their purpose is to
show thoughtful investigation and a genuine investment in the research problem.

**Conclusion.** Briefly summarize the project and discuss its broader impact. Explain how the work
could be continued, identify its current limitations, and describe problems that future work
should address.

**Contributions and AI usage acknowledgement.** If the project is completed in pairs, include a
contribution section explaining each team member's contributions. If you used AI tools, include an
AI usage acknowledgement section that clearly identifies the tools you used and explains how you
used them. The contribution and AI usage acknowledgement sections may appear after the four-page
report.
